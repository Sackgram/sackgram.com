Privacy Policy

Effective August 26, 2026 · Last updated September 18, 2026

This policy describes how Sackgram Labs, Inc. ("Sackgram", "we") handles information in the Sackgram application. We have tried to write it plainly, and to describe what actually happens rather than what sounds best.

The short version. Creating an account does not require a phone number or an email address. Message content is end-to-end encrypted and we cannot read it. We do still hold some information — a randomly assigned account identifier, who you are connected to, when messages were sent, and a few records we are required to keep. Sections 1 and 2 list all of it.

1. Information we collect

1.1 Account

  • A randomly assigned account identifier. Created when you first open the app. It is not derived from your device, your phone number, or anything about you.
  • Your SACKGRAM ID. The name you choose at sign-up, recorded so that no two people can hold the same one.
  • Your device's public key. Needed so other people can send you encrypted messages.
  • A record that you confirmed you are 18 or older, and, on Android devices where the operating system offers one, the result of an age signal provided by the app store. We keep the resulting decision as a compliance record. We do not keep your date of birth.
  • A record that you accepted these documents, including which language you read them in.

We do not ask for your name, phone number, email address, or contact list.

1.2 Messages and media

  • Message content is end-to-end encrypted. We store it only in encrypted form and cannot read it. The same applies to photos, videos and files you send.
  • We do see some information around messages, and it is important to be clear about this: which accounts are in a conversation, when messages were sent, roughly how large they are, and — for file attachments — the file name and size, which are not encrypted. The file name is stored with the message record, in readable form, so that the app can show you what the attachment is called before you download it. The file itself is encrypted and is stored under a random identifier rather than under its name.

1.3 Contacts and groups

The accounts you have connected with, the nickname you gave each of them (visible only to you), how the connection was made, and your group memberships.

1.4 Calls

We hold call records: who called whom, when the call started and ended, and whether it was answered.

Call audio is not recorded, and we cannot listen to it.

Setting up a call requires the two devices to exchange network addresses, which include IP addresses. Those addresses reach our servers — passing them between the two devices is how a call gets connected — and they are shared with the person you are calling. We delete them once the call ends.

Where a call was not answered, we record when that happened, so that the person who missed it can be shown that someone tried to reach them.

How the audio itself travels depends on the call, and there are two arrangements. In the first, the audio passes directly between the devices; where the network between them does not allow that, a relay server forwards it without being able to read it, which is an ordinary part of how internet calling works. In the second, a media server receives each person's audio and forwards it to the others — see section 3 for who operates it. In both, your device encrypts the audio before it is sent, so what passes through any server is encrypted; it is not recorded and we cannot listen to it.

1.5 Notifications

If you allow notifications, we store a push notification token for your device so we can tell it that a call is coming in. Notification delivery is handled by Google's push service.

1.6 Technical information

Our infrastructure providers receive IP addresses and connection information as an ordinary part of delivering network traffic, and may retain them in operational logs. This is normal for any internet service, and we mention it because a privacy policy that omitted it would be misleading.

We use a Google service that checks whether a request genuinely comes from the Sackgram app running on an ordinary device, so that our servers can turn away automated abuse. It examines the app and the device and returns a verdict to us; it does not tell us who you are, and we do not use it for anything else.

1.7 What we do not do

  • We do not sell or rent your personal information, and we do not share it with third parties for advertising.
  • We do not use advertising or tracking software development kits.
  • We do not build advertising or marketing profiles about you.
  • We do not read your messages, because we cannot.

2. Why we hold it

  • To run the service — deliver messages, connect calls, keep your contacts and groups.
  • To keep it working and secure — diagnose faults and prevent abuse of the service.
  • To meet legal obligations — the age and agreement records in section 1.1 exist for this reason and no other.

We do not use your information for any purpose other than the ones listed here.

3. Service providers

We use Google Cloud Platform and Firebase (Google LLC) for storage, authentication and push notifications. They process data on our instructions. If you buy a subscription, the purchase is handled by Apple or Google through their app stores — we do not receive your payment card details.

We use LiveKit (LiveKit, Inc.) for real-time voice infrastructure. Your device encrypts call audio before it is sent, so what passes through LiveKit is ciphertext and they cannot listen to it. They carry the connection, which means they receive the network addresses of the devices on a call, the identifiers of the accounts in it, and a short status string each device publishes — whether its loudspeaker is on, and whether Voice Privacy is on — which is how the other people on the call are shown those things.

We use RevenueCat (RevenueCat, Inc.) to keep track of whether an account holds a subscription. When you sign in, we give them the identifier of your account, so that a purchase made through the app store can be matched to it; they tell our servers which subscription that account holds and when it runs out. They do not receive your messages, your calls, or your payment card details.

We do not share your information with anyone else, except where section 5 applies.

4. How long we keep it

  • Messages stay until deleted. You can delete a message you sent, set a conversation to delete messages automatically, or remove a contact — which deletes that conversation's history for both people.
  • Call network addresses are deleted when the call ends.
  • The record that a call was not answered stays until the next unanswered call from the same person replaces it, or until the connection between you is removed.
  • Your account record and connections stay for as long as the account is in use.
  • Age and agreement records are kept for as long as we are required to be able to show that we took these steps.

5. When we may disclose information

We may disclose information we hold if we are legally required to, such as in response to a valid legal order. What we can produce is limited to what we actually have — the categories in section 1. We cannot produce the content of messages, because we do not hold it in readable form.

6. Your choices and rights

Depending on where you live, you may have the right to ask what information we hold about you, to have it corrected or deleted, or to object to how we use it. Contact us at info@sackgram.com.

Because accounts are not tied to a phone number or email address, we may be unable to verify that an account belongs to the person asking. Where we cannot verify ownership, we may have to decline the request. Most deletion can be done directly in the app — see Support.

7. Voice Privacy

Voice Privacy changes the pitch of your voice so that the person you are calling does not hear your natural voice. This section describes what it means for your information; what the feature is for, and the limits on how it may be used, are in section 4 of the Terms.

The processing happens on your device. Your voice is changed on your own phone, before anything is sent. We do not receive the original audio, and we do not receive the changed audio either — call audio is end-to-end encrypted and travels between the two devices.

We do not store your voice. Not the original, not the modified version. There is no recording of either on our servers, with or without this feature.

While a call is in progress our servers hold a flag indicating whether Voice Privacy is switched on, alongside the other call information described in section 1. It is there so the other participant can be shown that they are not hearing your real voice, and it is cleared when the call ends.

What it does not do. It does not make you anonymous. Changing the pitch of a voice does not change how quickly someone speaks, their rhythm, their accent or their choice of words, and it has no effect on any other information described in this policy.

8. Losing your device

Your conversations cannot be recovered — not by us, not by anyone. The keys that decrypt your messages exist only on your device and we hold no copy of them. This is a deliberate part of how Sackgram is built, not a limitation we are working to remove.

Recovering access to your account — your identity and your contacts, but never the contents of your conversations — is a separate question, and the answer depends on whether you set up a passkey.

If you set up a passkey, you can get back into your account on a new phone using your SACKGRAM ID and your fingerprint. Your account, your SACKGRAM ID and your contact list come back. Your conversations do not, for the reason above. Groups have to invite you again, because each member's group key is held for them on their own device.

You can also use it on the phone you already have, if you have forgotten your password.

If you did not set one up, there is no way back into the account. A passkey needs Android 9 or later, or iOS 16 or later, and a Google or Apple account — so on an older phone, or without one of those accounts, this route is not available and losing the device means losing the account.

Please read this before relying on Sackgram for anything you cannot afford to lose.

9. Age requirement

Sackgram is for people aged 18 and over. We do not knowingly collect information from anyone under 18.

How age is checked. At sign-up you enter your date of birth and we use it to confirm you are 18 or older. This is self-attested: we do not ask for identity documents and we do not check it against any official record. On some Android devices the app store can supply an additional age signal, which we use where it is available. As set out in section 1.1, we keep the resulting decision as a compliance record — we do not keep the date of birth itself.

If you believe someone under 18 has an account, email info@sackgram.com. We will close the account and delete the information we hold for it — the categories listed in section 1. Message content is end-to-end encrypted and we cannot read it, so what we are able to delete is limited to what we actually hold; anything already delivered to another person's device is on that device and beyond our reach.

10. International transfers

We operate in the United States and our service providers may process and store data there and in other countries. By using Sackgram you understand that your information may be processed outside the country where you live.

11. Changes to this policy

If we change this policy we will update the date at the top of this page, and for significant changes we will notify you in the app before the change takes effect.
